    This device

    Never used a proper camera?

    Three dials.
    That's it.

    A camera is a bucket you fill with light. Too little light and the photo is black. Too much and it is white. These three dials are the only ways to change how much gets in — and each one costs you something else.

    DIAL 1

    Aperture — f/2, f/8, f/16

    How wide the lens opens. Confusingly, a smaller number means a wider opening, so f/2 lets in far more light than f/16.

    SMALL NUMBER · f/2

    Bright. Background goes soft and blurry behind your subject.

    BIG NUMBER · f/16

    Dark. Everything front to back stays sharp.

    DIAL 2

    Shutter — 1/1000, 1/60, 1"

    How long the camera stares. 1/1000 is a blink; 1 second is a long look. This is the one that decides whether a moving subject is frozen or smeared.

    FAST · 1/1000

    Freezes anything. But barely any light gets in.

    SLOW · 1/15

    Lots of light. Anything moving turns to streaks.

    DIAL 3

    ISO — 100, 800, 6400

    How hard the camera strains to see. Raising it brightens a dark scene without touching the other two dials, but it adds grain — that speckly noise in night photos.

    LOW · 100

    Clean, smooth image. Needs plenty of light.

    HIGH · 6400

    Works in the dark. Looks grainy.

    THE ONLY RULE

    Turn one, fix it with another

    Every dial trades light for something else. Speed up the shutter to freeze a cyclist and the photo goes dark — so open the aperture or raise ISO to put that light back. That push and pull is the whole game.

    IF YOU ONLY REMEMBER ONE THING

    Keep the needle in the middle. The bar under the viewfinder is your light meter. Green in the centre means the brightness is right. Everything else is style.

    Camera basics · eight short steps

    One dial
    at a time

    Every other setting holds still. You only touch the one being taught, and you see the picture change live — no guessing which dial did what. Four steps on light, then focus, the lens, and the two ways a shutter can blur a frame.

    Course complete

    You know
    all five dials

    Shutter, ISO and aperture each move the same needle; focus and the lens decide what is sharp and how much of the world you keep. Different tools, one job — and now you have felt why.

    SHUTTERFreeze, blur, or shake
    APERTURELight and depth of field
    ISOBrightness at a cost
    FOCUSWhere the sharp zone sits
    LENSReach, shake, Rule of 500
